Interpretation:
- The hot detector is still significant, but the complete detection generator
  accounts for about 20% of sampled CPU time; the direct neighboring-cell
  lookup accounts for about 3%. A worker pool limited to detection would
  therefore have a bounded end-to-end benefit and add message-transfer cost.
- Timestamp validation is repeated for every record even though there are
  only 100,000 unique timestamps. Its refinement alone accounts for about
  21% of sampled CPU time. Caching successful timestamp validation by value
  is the strongest low-risk optimization to test next; it must retain the
  current validation for previously unseen timestamps.
- Parsing and validation remain meaningful costs. Benchmark any change against
  the same encounter counts and use repeated runs because these are sampled
  profiles and single-run wall-clock results.
